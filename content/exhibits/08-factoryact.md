# Factory Notice
year: 1833
by: Parliament (Factory Act)
summary: The first effective law limiting children's hours in textile factories.
tile: 17, 5
art: notice

## Page: Life on the factory floor
Factory work was long, loud, hot, and dangerous. Day shifts of 12-14 hours were common, six days a week. Mill owners hired many children because they were cheap and small enough to reach under moving machinery.

## Page: Reformers speak out
Parliamentary inquiries heard evidence about injuries, exhaustion, and harsh punishments. Reformers pushed for limits on working hours.

## Page: The Factory Act of 1833
The Act applied to textile mills. It banned the employment of children under 9, limited those aged 9-13 to 9 hours a day, and required 2 hours of schooling daily.

It also created paid FACTORY INSPECTORS to check the rules were kept, an important step towards modern workplace safety.

## Page: Who paid the price?
The 1833 Act was a start, but it had limits. It covered only textile mills, and just four inspectors had to check thousands of mills across the country. Some owners found ways around the rules.

Real change came slowly, through later laws.
