## Page: Moving goods and people
Raw cotton arrived at the port of Liverpool, and finished cloth had to travel back out to markets. Canals and horse-drawn wagons were slow, so mill owners and merchants wanted a faster link with Manchester.

## Page: Rocket (1829)
In October 1829 the Rainhill Trials tested locomotives for the new railway. Robert Stephenson's Rocket was the clear winner. It combined a multi-tube boiler, which raised steam quickly, with a blast-pipe that strengthened the fire.

Its top speed was close to 30 miles per hour, far faster than a horse.

## Page: Liverpool & Manchester Railway (1830)
The line opened on 15 September 1830. It was the first intercity railway, the first to rely entirely on locomotives, and the first to run a scheduled passenger service.

It carried goods as well as passengers, and it became a model for railways around the world.

## Page: Who paid the price?
The opening day ended in tragedy. William Huskisson, a Member of Parliament, was struck by the Rocket at Parkside and died that day.

Railways were built by thousands of laborers known as navvies, whose work with picks, shovels and gunpowder was hard and dangerous.
