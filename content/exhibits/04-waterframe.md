# Water Frame
year: 1769
by: Richard Arkwright
summary: A water-powered spinning machine that made strong thread and needed a factory.
tile: 27, 7
art: waterframe

## Page: Strong thread by machine
Richard Arkwright patented his spinning frame in 1769. It drew cotton out between sets of rollers turning at different speeds, then twisted it into thread strong enough for the warp.

## Page: Power from the river
The frame was too large and heavy to turn by hand. Arkwright first used horses, but horse power proved too costly, so he turned to a WATER WHEEL.

Mills therefore had to stand beside fast-flowing rivers, and workers had to travel to the machines.

## Page: The first factories
In 1771 Arkwright built a water-powered mill at Cromford in Derbyshire. UNESCO now recognizes the Derwent Valley mills as the birthplace of the factory system: purpose-built buildings that housed new machinery, power, and workers together.

Before this, most cloth was made in homes and workshops.

## Page: Who paid the price?
Women and children made up much of the workforce, often working long hours. Arkwright's mills turned out cheap, strong thread, but they also changed how people lived.

Work ran by the bell and the clock, not by the sun and the seasons. People who once set their own pace at home now followed the factory's schedule.
