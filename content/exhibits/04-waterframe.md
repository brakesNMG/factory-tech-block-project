# Water Frame
year: 1769
by: Richard Arkwright
summary: A water-wheel-driven spinning machine that made strong thread and needed a factory.
tile: 27, 7
art: waterframe

## Page: Strong thread by machine
Richard Arkwright patented the water frame in 1769. It used sets of rollers turning at different speeds to stretch and twist cotton into thread strong enough for warp.

## Page: Power from the river
The frame was too big and heavy to turn by hand, so it was driven by a WATER WHEEL.

That meant mills had to be built beside fast-flowing rivers, and the workers had to come to the machines.

## Page: The first factories
Arkwright opened a water-powered mill at Cromford, Derbyshire, in 1771. Machines, power, and workers under one roof, working set hours: this is the FACTORY SYSTEM.

Before this, most textile work was done at home.

## Page: Who paid the price?
Arkwright's mills ran day and night and needed many workers. Many were children, and some were poor apprentices sent from workhouses and bound to the mill for years.

Mills ran by the bell and the clock, not by the sun or the seasons. People who once set their own pace at home now followed the factory's schedule.
