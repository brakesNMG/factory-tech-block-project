## Page: Now the weavers fall behind
With the jenny and water frame, spinning had become cheap and plentiful. Now hand-loom weavers were the slow step.

## Page: Cartwright's loom (1785)
Edmund Cartwright, a clergyman, patented a loom that threw the shuttle and beat the weft with power from an engine or wheel.

His first design was clumsy. Other inventors improved it over the next decades.

## Page: The decline of hand-loom weaving
By the 1820s-30s power looms were common in Lancashire mills. One young worker could mind several looms.

Cloth got much cheaper, but hand-loom weavers' wages collapsed. Some protested, and groups such as the Luddites even smashed machines.

## Page: Who paid the price?
In 1826, crowds of hand-loom weavers in Lancashire smashed hundreds of power looms as their wages and work disappeared, and soldiers were called out.

Power looms also hired many women and children to mind several looms, often at lower pay than skilled weavers had earned.
