## Page: Literature: the novelist
Charles Dickens (1812-1870) wrote "Hard Times" in 1854. It is set in Coketown, a made-up factory town. He visited Preston during a mill strike to research it.

It was written twenty years after this game's 1834 setting, but it looks at the kind of town the inventions had built. The passage is from Book 1, chapter 5, "The Keynote".

## Page: The passage (part 1)
"It was a town of red brick, or of brick that would have been red if the smoke and ashes had allowed it; but as matters stood, it was a town of unnatural red and black like the painted face of a savage. It was a town of machinery and tall chimneys, out of which interminable serpents of smoke trailed themselves for ever and ever, and never got uncoiled."

## Page: The passage (part 2)
"It had a black canal in it, and a river that ran purple with ill-smelling dye, and vast piles of building full of windows where there was a rattling and a trembling all day long, and where the piston of the steam-engine worked monotonously up and down, like the head of an elephant in a state of melancholy madness."

- Charles Dickens, "Hard Times" (1854)

## Page: Technique: simile and metaphor
A SIMILE compares two things using "like" or "as". Dickens says the piston moves "like the head of an elephant in a state of melancholy madness". The engine seems alive, huge and trapped, and its endless up-and-down motion feels sad.

"Serpents of smoke" is a METAPHOR. The smoke is not a snake, but the image makes it threatening.

## Page: Technique: diction and colour
DICTION means word choice. Dickens picks negative words: "unnatural", "interminable", "monotonously", "melancholy madness".

His COLOUR IMAGERY shows nature stained by industry: red brick turned black, a black canal, a river running purple with dye. The "painted face" comparison makes the town seem ugly and false, though the word "savage" also shows the attitudes of Dickens's own time.

## Page: Technique: repetition and tone
Many sentences start "It was a town of..." or "It had...". This REPETITION copies the sameness of the town and its machines. Nothing changes, "for ever and ever".

The TONE is gloomy and satirical. Dickens exaggerates on purpose to criticise a society that treated people like parts of a machine.
